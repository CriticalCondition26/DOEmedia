## 5. The single weakest spot per page, rewritten

### v4-1 Wine Rep: weakest spot is the MECHANISM ("Why someone who has tried everything picked this one")
The headline promises the verdict of someone who has tried every cure. The section that should pay that off says "Water does water. A sports drink does sugar. Coffee does coffee." That's a slogan, not a reason. It never says why her old fixes fell short, so the reader can't believe this one is different.
Structure: golden thread #26, "Say their doubt out loud" (Sugarman, letter 015: "Do those BluBlockers really work?"), then thread #13, "Confess before you claim" (Crest, letter 285).

Current: `Most hangover fixes do one thing. Water does water. A sports drink does sugar. Coffee does coffee.`
Replace:
`You're probably thinking what anyone in her job would think: it's another salty drink with "hangover" on the label. Here's the difference she found. Everything she had already tried covers one thing. Plain water has no sodium at all. A leading sports drink has about one ninth of the electrolytes in one Weekend Recovery stick. Coffee gets you moving and does nothing for your stomach. So on the second morning of a trip, you end up stacking three or four of them and hoping one works.`
(The founder paragraph that follows then reads as the answer: they stacked five things by hand, and the stick replaces the stack.)

### v4-2 3 A.M. Scroll: weakest spot is the MECHANISM (the L-theanine section)
The opening is the best in the set. Then the page has to explain why a hangover stick would help with dread, and it falls back on a made-up green tea line and the same founder story as pages 1, 3 and 4. The reader's real question is never answered: *what in here is for the 3 A.M. feeling, and why isn't it in what I already drink?*
Structure: golden thread #9, "The ruled-out guess" (Lampropoulos, letter 034: "Secret ingredient in Italian food... (No, it's not garlic!)"), used in the subhead (must-fix 2.1), then a plain answer.

Current: `L-theanine is an amino acid found in tea. It's the reason a cup of green tea feels calmer than a cup of coffee. Waterboy describes its job plainly: to calm your nerves and restlessness.`
Replace:
`Step five was L-theanine, an amino acid found in tea leaves. Each stick of Weekend Recovery has 200mg of it, and the number is printed on the front of the pouch. Waterboy describes its job in six words: to calm your nerves and restlessness. That's the step a glass of water or a typical sports drink leaves out, and it's the one aimed at 3 A.M.`
(Use only after the 200mg dose is confirmed. Until then, use the fallback in must-fix 2.2.)

### v4-3 Prescription: weakest spot is the LEAD (headline through the first David quote)
The lead builds up bartenders as hangover experts, which no review says, and it never names the reader's problem. A cold reader learns that a couple is popular at bars but not why they should care. Fix: lead with the morning, which is what the proof proves, and make being asked the reward.
Structure: golden thread #24, "Two men, one difference" (Martin Conroy, letter 022: "But there was a difference."), closed with thread #12, "Applause from your peers" (letter 574 L30: "Where did you get these, and how can I get some?").

Current (three paragraphs): `Bartenders have seen every hangover trick there is.` / `They watch the same people walk in Friday, Saturday and Sunday. They know who's going to be fine tomorrow and who's going to be calling in sick. And they have heard every "trust me, this works" pitch from every regular who ever leaned on the bar.` / `So it says something when the people pouring the drinks start asking a customer for his stash.`
Replace (three paragraphs):
`Two people sit at the same bar on Saturday night. Same last call, same ride home.`
`At 10 a.m. Sunday, one of them is on the couch with the blinds shut and a group chat they're afraid to open. The other one is at brunch asking who wants to split the pancakes. Ask the second one what's different and you'll hear the same answer every time: two sticks, one before the night ends and one when the alarm goes off.`
`David T. J. is the second one. And it shows, because the people who pour his drinks have started asking him for one.`
Then current: `That customer is David T. J. He and his partner go out a lot. Here's how he put it in his review on waterboy.com:` Replace: `Here's how he put it in his review on waterboy.com:`

### v4-4 Remember When: weakest spot is the MECHANISM ("What changed")
The headline promises an answer to "why can't I do both anymore?" The section answers it with "The night didn't change. Your mornings did. There's more on the calendar, less time to recover." That's the vague, no-paradox writing the owner rejected. The twist is sitting right there and isn't stated: the problem was never the hangover, it was losing the hours you used to sleep it off.
Structure: golden thread #7, "Right? Wrong!" (Bencivenga, letter 080 GH-12: "You're always safe taking the standard deduction, right? Wrong!").

Current: `The night didn't change. Your mornings did. There's more on the calendar, less time to recover, and no more sleeping until 2 p.m. to make up for it. The hangover you used to sleep off now has to be handled while you're awake.`
Replace:
`Most people think the answer is more sleep. Sleep it off, right? Wrong. You don't have the sleep anymore. At 22 you could stay in bed until 2 p.m. and call it recovery. Now Sunday has a 9 a.m. in it: the dog, the run, the soccer game, the parent who calls early. The night out is the same night it always was. What's gone is the 12 hours you used to have to get over it. So the hangover you used to sleep through now has to be handled while you're awake, and that's a different job.`
