Remember When You Could Do Saturday Night and Still Have Sunday? The Two-Stick Habit People Past 25 Use to Get Both Back.

At 22 you went out Saturday and played pickup Sunday morning. Now one wedding takes out a whole weekend. Here's what changed, and what the people who still do both keep in their bag.

WBy the Waterboy team · 5 min read

There was a time when Saturday night was free.

You went out. You stayed out. You got four hours of sleep, ate a bacon egg and cheese, and played pickup basketball at 10 a.m. Sunday was just more weekend.

Then, somewhere around 25, the bill started arriving.

Now two drinks feel like five. A birthday dinner turns into a lost Sunday on the couch, blinds shut, phone at 3%. You sit out the morning run, the farmers market, your nephew's soccer game. And the worst part isn't Sunday. It's the Monday you walk into still not right.

Waterboy's founders did the math on that trade, and they put it better than we could:

"Just traded 4 hours of fun for 24 hours of misery. Not a good investment."

Most people respond to that math one of two ways. They stop going out, and their friends stop inviting them. Or they keep going out and quietly give up Sundays.

There's a third way, and the people who use it aren't any younger than you.

What changed

The night didn't change. Your mornings did. There's more on the calendar, less time to recover, and no more sleeping until 2 p.m. to make up for it. The hangover you used to sleep off now has to be handled while you're awake.

Tori S., a wine and spirits portfolio manager who tastes wine for a living, said it in her review: "I have tried all the "cures"....I am also not in my early twenties anymore, let's just say that."

So the people who still do both stopped trying to sleep it off and started handling it on purpose.
